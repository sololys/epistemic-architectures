## 📐 1. The Geometry of Quantum Reality

Every quantum state is represented as a vector inside a special mathematical space called a **Hilbert Space** ($\mathcal{H}$). Unlike ordinary 3D space, a Hilbert space can have infinitely many dimensions while still allowing distances, angles, and probabilities to be calculated consistently.

## 🔬 2. Inner Product $\langle \psi, \phi \rangle$

This measures how similar two quantum states are:

$$\langle \psi, \phi \rangle \in \mathbb{C}$$

* $\psi$ = First quantum state (vector)
* $\phi$ = Second quantum state (vector)
* $\langle \psi, \phi \rangle$ = Overlap between the two states
* **Large Overlap:** The states are very similar ($|\langle \psi, \phi \rangle| \approx 1$).
* **Zero Overlap (Orthogonal):** The states are completely independent and mutually exclusive ($\langle \psi, \phi \rangle = 0$).

---

## 📏 3. Length of a Quantum State (Norm)

$$\|\psi\| = \sqrt{\langle \psi, \psi \rangle}$$

* $\psi$ = Quantum state
* $\langle \psi, \psi \rangle$ = Inner product of the state with itself
* $\sqrt{\cdot}$ = Square root
* $\|\psi\|$ = Length (norm) of the vector

> **Betydning:** Normen forteller hvor stor kvantetilstanden er, og brukes til å beregne sannsynligheter via Born-regelen ($P = |\langle \phi | \psi \rangle|^2$). For alle fysiske lukkede systemer er $\|\psi\| = 1$.

---

## 🌐 4. Infinite-Dimensional Stability

A Hilbert Space behaves like an infinite-dimensional version of normal geometry. Instead of describing the position of objects, it describes every possible quantum state of particles such as electrons and photons:

$$|\psi\rangle = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n |e_n\rangle, \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |c_n|^2 = 1$$

Its complete mathematical structure guarantees that quantum calculations remain stable, even when infinitely many possibilities are involved.
